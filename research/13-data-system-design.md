# 13 — Data-system design review

Reviewed 5 September 2026. Scope: the current `ARCHITECTURE_PROPOSAL.md`; Kleppmann's author-published DDIA research articles on dual writes and fencing; the caching/materialized-view sections of his database-inside-out transcript; and SQLite isolation documentation. This is a review of those accessible materials, not a claim to have read all of DDIA or *Making Sense of Stream Processing*. The articles supply the permitted derived-data/log alternative.

**Recommendation:** retain one SQLite database, ordinary service functions, a durable jobs table, and initially one processing worker. For approximately 500 records and two development days, transactional correctness and explicit readiness states matter more than adding a broker, cache server, graph database, or distributed locks. The following are concrete design recommendations inferred from the sources and the draft.

1. **Define the commit boundary.** The draft's step 6 commits memories before step 7 updates search representations, leaving an underspecified failure window. Commit accepted assertions, evidence, relations, lifecycle changes, lexical-index maintenance, and pending embedding jobs together. Persist embedding results later only against their expected revision; update job completion in that same transaction. This adapts Kleppmann's argument that independently updating related stores creates partial-failure and ordering hazards. His article also explains why asynchronous consumers do not automatically provide read-your-writes. [Using logs to build a solid data infrastructure](https://martin.kleppmann.com/2015/05/27/logs-for-data-infrastructure.html)

2. **Make retry effects idempotent.** Require unique import identities scoped by user and upstream record ID/revision; a content hash detects unchanged input but should not merge separate events containing identical text. Uniquely identify a processing job by source revision, stage, and configuration version. Retrying a committed job must return its recorded result without inserting another assertion/evidence set. Model calls may repeat after a crash; do not promise exactly-once external execution or identical regenerated model output.

3. **Prevent late jobs from undoing corrections.** Capture source revision, relevant memory revisions, and exclusion generation before a model call. Within the final write transaction, re-read and compare them; discard/requeue stale work. A processing lease alone cannot authorize a late write. If multiple workers are later necessary, atomically claim jobs and validate an attempt token at commit. This applies the storage-enforced fencing principle to local jobs without importing distributed lock infrastructure. [How to do distributed locking](https://martin.kleppmann.com/2016/02/08/how-to-do-distributed-locking.html)

4. **Specify what incomplete indexing means.** Keep lexical reads usable immediately after commit. Dense retrieval must join vectors to current permitted revisions; missing vectors mean explicitly incomplete dense coverage. Never serve an obsolete vector as current. Rebuild from a consistent revision snapshot and publish only after validation. Retain the draft's zero persistent answer cache; if added later, scope/generation keys must also cover new imports and embedding-readiness changes. Cached copies are derived views with invalidation obligations. [Turning the database inside-out](https://martin.kleppmann.com/2015/03/04/turning-the-database-inside-out.html)

5. **Separate history from erasure.** Ordinary revision preserves earlier evidence; forgetting must also purge rejected candidates, copied excerpts, prompts/responses, traces, and derived aliases containing the forgotten material. Rebuild only from surviving permitted evidence. Content-free exclusions prevent reprocessing known deleted IDs. State the boundary for original import files, backups, and provider retention; database deletion alone cannot establish their erasure.

6. **Use short database transactions.** SQLite serializes writers; WAL permits concurrent snapshot readers. Use separate connections and bounded busy retries. Do not hold read/write transactions over model calls. Revalidate answer evidence against a fresh generation before release, because an old read transaction will not see a concurrent deletion. [Isolation in SQLite](https://sqlite.org/isolation.html)

Acceptance scenarios: crash between stages, duplicate import/retry, correction during extraction, deletion during answering, and partial embedding rebuild. Each must preserve evidence integrity and prevent stale material from returning.
